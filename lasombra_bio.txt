Chapter I. - What was taken
Juliette was born the sole daughter of a family that had long since ceased to measure its fortunes in anything as vulgar as money.
Her father had built his empire through a combination of extraordinary intelligence, ambition, and an almost unnerving ability to understand the people with whom he dealt. He could look across a boardroom and know which man would fold first, which woman could be persuaded, and precisely how much pressure would be required before either realized they had lost. His success had made the family wealthy enough to not need for anything, have prestige enough that doors opened when asked nicely enough.
Her mother was different.
Beautiful, elegant, and seemingly effortless in her refinement, she had become the living ornament of the family. Her beauty had been one of the things that first attracted her husband, and over the years it had become almost inseparable from the family's public image. Their progeny inherited much of that beauty.
From her father, however, Juliette inherited something far more consequential.
That instinctive desire and ability to read the room. To understand the people for what they were worth. To determine who would be defeated, could be persuaded, or intimidated into submission.
Her education only sharpened the distinction.
She was given every advantage her family could provide: the finest schools, private tutors, languages, economics, history, etiquette, and eventually an understanding of business to an extent she could surpass her peers at the time. And Juliette absorbed it all readily. Yet what fascinated her most was not the accumulation of knowledge, nor even the accumulation of wealth.
It was influence.
And so she began experimenting.
At first, the experiments were theoretical in nature. Youthful curiosity. A suggestion here, an introduction there. A carefully chosen piece of information passed to one proprietor while another was encouraged to believe something entirely different. The young woman learned how quickly confidence could be manufactured, how easily an ambitious owner could be convinced to expand too quickly, and how little pressure was required to turn a sound decision into a disastrous one.
Then she began doing it deliberately.
She would identify a struggling business and study it as though it were a puzzle. She learned who made the decisions, who influenced them, who could be flattered, who could be frightened, and who could be made to believe that an idea had been theirs from the beginning.
Sometimes she intervened only to see whether she could. Sometimes, a modest investment beforehand. It was a hobby at first. Consulting friends’ business ideas. Aiding small enterprises with investments.
She realized there was good money in it. And she had the knack for it. Yet she also found out that there were a great many layers above what she only saw. So she began pursuing a career in that direction, to no one's surprise. 
The following years turned her into a successful businesswoman. A dreaded liquidator. People slowly started to degrade into resources in her mind. Yet in the process, Juliette saw something beautiful. She didn’t feel it to be malicious, but a necessary evil. In order to grow, you have to weed out the chaff.
And when the inevitable collapse came, she watched things collapse. She passed protesting workers in a high end vehicle. Turned a blind eye on the suffering of those who failed to grasp their own destiny and became servants of those who ruled over them. 
Every success taught her something. Every failure taught her more.
Wealth, beauty, education, influence, and a future that seemed almost impossibly secure.
She did not yet know that there was a difference between controlling someone's life and destroying it. Something would eventually be taught to her.
That someone would begin by taking away everything she had. Piece by piece. Dollar by dollar. Friends. Family. That feeling of security, pulled from under her feet. 
It happened because she turned her attention to the wrong business, Juliette and for all she knew anyone else - raising the attention of someone…something she had not expected to. It was a simple warehouse - didn’t even seem too profitable for her. She hesitated to even pick up the job. It was going to be over in a month or two. Maybe have a vacation’s worth of cash to grab from it. Yet it became a battle she could not ever see coming.
And when there was nothing left to take, yet Juliette still fiercely found ways to refuse bending the knee. If there is nothing left to take from her, she will take it from another source. Find a way. And only when she had lost everything is when an opportunity revealed itself…or rather himself.
Chapter II. - The curse of a gift
It all started small. Even slipped her attention, initially. Small influxes of cash to the business she was about to demolish. Keeping them afloat long enough to ride out the crisis. Investors, business partners estranging, going missing. Lawsuits, accusations.
The losses, humiliations slowly but surely became deliberate. Personal. The pressure became almost impossible to endure.
And still, Juliette refused to bend. She adapted.
Yet whoever was responsible remained invisible. It became a source of her drive almost to an obscene level. More than anything, it fascinated her - and infuriated her. Both gave her a flame of determination. She saw it as a game. And the mouse wanted to find out who the cat was.
She knew the game. At least she thought she did. There were patterns. Money could be tracked. People could be persuaded to speak. Tested. They would slip with the right buttons being pushed.

Yet each time she thought she had finally found the hand behind the curtain, Juliette discovered another layer that derailed her investigation. This game was different - perhaps not even a game. There was doubt in her mind - but she was determined to find out who dared pushing her buttons.
The patterns started to converge. Puzzle pieces forming a shape. And it was hers. She stood on a board where she felt to be the pawn. But even realizing that the goal of the game was her fall, she refused to bend the knee. Attempted to adapt time and time again. Changing business strategies. Shifting focus. All met with the same inescapable fate. 

Even family and friends were fair game. Many went missing. Others became wary of her. Distanced. The reasons were often shifty enough to raise suspicion. Or maybe at this point Juliette became paranoid, driving them away. Perhaps both were true. The few leads she found, people - disappeared without a single trace.
Yet whatever else had been stripped from her, her pride remained. Battered, yes. But not broken. That was the moment when /he/ finally appeared.
The man did not present himself as a monster. Not at first. There was no theatrical revelation, no need for intimidation. He simply stepped out of the shadows as though he had always been there. And he invited her to a meeting, for he had a proposal.
In a sense, he had been, for a while.
He told her what she had already begun to suspect: that the destruction of her life had been his doing. Every carefully arranged failure. Every severed connection. Every door closed in her face.
The test was over and she had passed. He admired that even in the face of something so otherworldly, Juliette didn’t relent. She looked for the fine print. She queried what opportunities were to be found.
He admired the resolve that had allowed her to endure what would have destroyed most mortals. He admired the intelligence with which she had attempted to uncover him. And, perhaps most dangerously, he had become obsessed with the beauty that had survived it all. Though this last part, Theodore never revealed.
Introducing himself as Theodore Francisco de Haro, a nobleman, per his words - and the bringer of an offer. He took all so Juliette could find her true calling. And gain so much more than mere wealth. Power. Influence. Toppling the highest towers.

And after everything she had lost, the promise was intoxicating. She had spent her mortal life learning how to manipulate people. Her sire offered her the means to become something far greater than a manipulator. And he offered to be able to do that, from behind the scenes. Without the constrictions of law, he offered her the shadows themselves. 
Theodore wanted to preserve Juliette. Not merely the physical beauty of the woman, though it was perhaps one of the strongest factors in his decision. But the work of art he believed her to be.
There was a price, of course. There always was.
After everything that had been taken from her, immortality did not sound like a curse. It sounded like compensation. So she accepted. How could she not?  She gave him her blood. And in return, he gave her eternity. 
Yet she did not yet understand what else he had given her. The hunger. The darkness. The chains hidden within the blood.
Nor did she understand that the most profound part of the Embrace was not that her heart had stopped beating. It was that something inside her had begun to answer when he called. The freshly formed cainite believed she had accepted a gift. Yet she would spend the years that followed made her question the choice. She made a bargain with the devil..

Chapter III. - Obsession
Her education as Kindred began with the rules. The Traditions, the Masquerade, the clans and their ancient rivalries, the sects and their endless wars. She learned how to feed without leaving a trail, how to recognize the Beast before it took hold, and how to navigate a society where centuries of experience could hide a stake behind a polite smile. Theodore taught her that the Kindred were not simply predators, but predators who had built an entire civilization around concealing what they were. She found herself fascinated. The rules were merely another system to understand, another chessgame of power to master.
Yet these lessons went beyond mere survival. He refined her manners, her speech, her posture, even the smallest details of her appearance. He further educated her how to enter a room and command attention without asking for it, how to make others feel that their decisions were their own. He was meticulous in his instruction as well as punishments, but increasingly, Juliette noticed that Theodore’s interest was not merely that of a sire shaping his childe. He watched her with an intensity that went beyond pride. He remembered every detail of her mortal beauty and seemed fascinated by what the Embrace had preserved.
She began to understand that she was not merely his childe, nor simply his protégé. She was something he considered precious—something to be protected, cultivated, and preserved. Something he considered his. At first, the attention pleased her. After all, she had been chosen because she was exceptional. But gradually, the distinction between being cherished and being possessed became harder to ignore. He decided whom she should meet, what she should learn, and which dangers she should be kept from. Whenever she questioned his judgment, there was always the same answer: not yet.
She did not yet understand why. Nor did she understand whether he was protecting her because he cared for her, or because he could not bear to see his most beautiful creation damaged. She only knew that his fascination with her was becoming - or perhaps already has been an obsession - and that, beneath all his lessons about the freedom and power of the night, there was a quiet assumption that she belonged to him. Which made her begin to question the gift, perhaps for the first time. But certainly not the last.
Chapter IV. - Abandoned
The news came as a surprise - an unwelcome, carried through whispers before it ever reached her. Xanathos - a name she only was vaguely aware of. An infernalist - had fallen alongside their empire, which had held Pittsburgh in its grip for years. Those who had served him were dead, scattered, or desperately attempting to disappear. Among them was her sire. Details were scarce. Juliette had not witnessed it. There had been no final words, no farewell, no opportunity to ask the questions that had begun to trouble her. One night, he was simply gone.
Then came the silence.
She waited first. Someone would respond. Surely there would be retribution. Surely someone would come looking for those responsible, if only to demonstrate that such a death could not go unanswered. Nothing came. No vengeance. No explanation. No hand reached out. The silence became more deafening with each night passing. Slowly, an ugly possibility crept onto her thoughts: perhaps her sire had not been betrayed merely by Xanatos. Perhaps he had simply been deemed expendable by those who had once called him brother.
And then she began to wonder what that made her. If they had not lifted a finger to save him, what reason would they have to protect her? If they knew she existed, would they consider her a loose end? A witness? Another piece of his legacy that needed to be removed? For the first time since her Embrace, fear began to take root—not fear of the Beast, nor of the mortal world, but of the very sect she belonged to. She had spent years learning that loyalty was strength. Now she was beginning to suspect that, within the Sabbat, loyalty only lasted as long as one remained useful. Not too unfamiliar. And if this was the case, businesses that show weakness tend to be consumed. So she had to keep swimming.
Remembering lessons told by Theodore about the Camarilla - though at the time, he had dismissed it as part of the lesson in the endless games of kindred politics. Now the memory returned with a different weight. The Camarilla had laws. Hierarchies. Domains. Alliances. Consequences. Perhaps it was not the freedom she desired. It could even spell death. Yet taking no action was almost certain to bare no fruit to a fresh bloodsucker as her, despite the cautions voiced by Theodore. Her loyalties did not change in a single moment, though they were not solidified by decades either, they were malleable still. And so they shifted quietly, almost imperceptibly, as she began looking toward the Tower - both mentally preparing herself for a rude welcome, and beginning an investigation through the few contacts she did have. And she could offer something in return. Those who refused to enact vengeance on her sire. Despite their dubious connection, Juliette wanted justice at the very least. If she could gather the power to do so.
Chapter V. - In the shadows of the tower
Juliette did not simply walk away from the Sabbat. She disappeared from it. There was a sliver of hope that they didn’t even realize she was existing. The sword of caine was not as strict with their tracking of embraces as their counterpart.
For a time, she kept to the fringes of kindred society, relying upon the skills her sire had taught her. She listened more than she spoke, watched who came and went, and began seeking those who lived between the boundaries of the great sects. Anarchs, independents, caitiff, smugglers of information and others who had little reason to care which banner flew above the city. None of them could offer her safety. Not against the Sabbat. But they offered something more valuable: intel.
Piece by piece, she began assembling a path. A name here. A favor there. Information purchased with secrets, promises, or carefully chosen manipulation. She learned who could be approached, who could not, and which doors were best opened indirectly. She never asked for admission. Not yet. She was not foolish enough to announce herself to a society that might regard her as a traitor, a liability, or worse—a Sabbat spy. Instead, she sought a way to place herself before someone who could make the decision.
The closer she came, the more uncertain she became of what awaited her. The Camarilla was no sanctuary. She knew enough of the kindred to understand that. The Tower had its own predators, its own games, its own hierarchies and cruelties.A system in which power came with obligations, and where even the death of a neonate could carry consequences if the right person had reason to care.
Eventually, one of the threads she had been following finally led somewhere. A name. A meeting. A door that, if opened, might lead her into the Ivory Tower. For the first time since her sire's death, she had a choice that was truly her own. She could remain in the shadows and continue running, or step forward and place herself at the mercy of those she hoped might become her shield. She had spent years learning how to manipulate the world from behind the curtain.
Now, for the first time, she was about to step onto the stage.
